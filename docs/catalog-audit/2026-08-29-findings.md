# Full-catalog quality audit — 2026-08-29

Triggered by the user spot-checking Sanderson's books and finding real
errors (Rhythm of War's `morally_grey_protagonist` mistag, missing
species tropes). Rather than fix Sanderson alone, ran the same review
across all 167 books in the catalog: 14 background agents, each auditing
~12 books directly against the schema and their own knowledge of the
book, connected straight to the local Postgres DB with write access to
fix what they found. Two batches hit a transient session rate limit
mid-run and were cleanly retried. All fixes applied to local, then
synced to the hosted DB via 167 scoped per-book statements (never a
blanket table wipe) — verified matching row counts on both
(167 books / 167 book_dna / 1166 tropes / 350 content warnings, up from
867 tropes / 336 warnings before this pass).

Full list of every SQL statement executed: `full-audit-sync-2026-08-29.sql`
in this directory.

## The recurring error the audit was built to catch

`morally_grey_protagonist` had been misapplied to several ensemble-cast
books where the actual POV characters read as straightforwardly heroic,
not morally ambiguous — the exact pattern the user caught in Rhythm of
War. Found and fixed the same error independently in:

- **Good Omens** (Crowley/Aziraphale/Adam/Anathema/Newt — no single
  protagonist, overwhelmingly heroic outcome)
- **Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince** (no other HP book carries
  this tag; looked like a stray mistag)
- **A Game of Thrones** (Ned/Catelyn/Sansa/Arya/Bran/Jon are all
  straightforwardly honorable in this volume; replaced with `anti_hero`
  for Tyrion specifically, since he's this book's actual grey-ish figure)
- **Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell** (Strange/Norrell read as flawed,
  hubristic academics, not morally grey anti-heroes)
- **The Alloy of Law** and **The Bands of Mourning** (reverted my own
  addition from earlier tonight — Wax is a guilt-ridden but consistently
  principled lawman archetype, not genuinely morally ambiguous; good
  sign the audit does real independent review rather than rubber-stamping)

...and correctly **kept** where a genuinely grey POV character justifies
it: Circe, Jade City, The Way of Kings/The Well of Ascension (Shallan/Vin
specifically), Warbreaker, Words of Radiance, Six of Crows, Leviathan
Wakes (via Miller), Interview with the Vampire, Kings of Paradise (Ruka),
Artemis (Jazz), Elantris (Hrathen), Tender Is the Flesh.

## Books that were essentially untagged

- **Bird Box** had zero tropes at all — added `post_apocalyptic`,
  `sudden_apocalypse_event`, `survivalist_ingenuity`.
- **House of Leaves** had zero tropes — added `major_character_death`,
  but flagged that this book genuinely has very little SFF-vocabulary
  overlap (it's experimental literary horror, not genre fantasy) and no
  trope exists for its actual central conceit (impossible/shifting
  architecture).
- **The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy** had exactly one trope.
- **One Hundred Years of Solitude** had two, despite being an
  extraordinarily trope-dense novel.

## Real factual corrections (not just missing tags)

- **He Who Fights with Monsters**: `reincarnated_protagonist` was
  factually wrong — verified via web search that Jason Asano is
  transported while alive (accidental summoning), not reincarnated.
  Corrected to `isekai`.
- **Frankenstein**: `person` field corrected `mixed` → `first` (all
  three nested narrators use first person; `mixed` requires an actual
  person-switch, which doesn't happen here).
- **Exit Strategy**: removed `cybernetic_enhancement` (Murderbot is a
  manufactured construct from the start, not an augmented human) and
  `underdog_rising` (already lethally powerful from book one).
- **Death's End**: removed `hive_mind` — the Trisolarans have
  transparent/public thought, which is a different concept from a
  unified collective consciousness.
- **City of Bones**: removed `secret_royalty` — Shadowhunter society has
  no monarchy; Clary's heritage is about bloodline/rune ability.
- **Divergent**: removed `chosen_one` — no prophecy/destiny framing
  exists; already correctly captured by `hidden_talent_prodigy`.

## `stakes_scope` global/cosmic consistency pass

The global-vs-cosmic boundary clarified during the `personal_stakes`
work (cosmic = beyond one universe/reality, not just "very large") got
applied as a real consistency check across the catalog:

- **A Darker Shade of Magic**: `global` → `cosmic` (the schema's own
  documentation cites this exact book's parallel Londons as the
  canonical cosmic example — the original tagging had contradicted its
  own citation).
- **Mistborn: The Final Empire**: `regional` → `global` (matches the
  Dune/Foundation precedent for a single, all-encompassing empire).
- **Hyperion**, **The Dark Forest**: `cosmic` → `global` (galaxy-spanning
  single-universe conflicts, not multiverse-scale).
- **Neuromancer**: `global` → `regional` (the actual plot risk is one
  corporate dynasty's fate, not civilization-wide).
- **One Hundred Years of Solitude**: `intimate` → `regional` (the book's
  arc is the founding-to-erasure of an entire town, not a small cast).

## Genre-fit issues newly surfaced

**The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo** is tagged `genre: {sci_fi}` despite
zero speculative content — both `magic_system_hardness` and
`scifi_hardness` are already correctly `na`, directly contradicting the
genre tag. Not fixed (the schema's genre enum is closed to
`[sci_fi, fantasy]`, and neither is actually correct — forcing `fantasy`
would be an equally wrong fix). This joins **The Silent Patient**
(previously found with the same issue and corrected to `genre: {}`) as a
case needing a product decision: exclude from the catalog, or open the
genre enum to allow "not really SFF" as an honest answer.

## Vocabulary gaps flagged (not applied — logged for deliberate review)

- **No trope for "multiple sentient alien species" in a pure sci-fi
  book** — `multiple_fantasy_species` is fantasy-coded by name and
  intent, so it doesn't fit The Long Way to a Small, Angry Planet's or
  Old Man's War's several alien species despite the identical shape of
  gap. Flagged independently by two different agents auditing unrelated
  parts of the catalog — the same kind of repeated-signal case that
  justified adding `embedded_system_text` earlier. Strongest new
  candidate from this round.
- No "alternate_history" trope (would help The Man in the High Castle).
- No vocabulary for Gethenian ambisexuality/kemmer biology (The Left
  Hand of Darkness's most famous conceit has no home).
- No "revenge plot" trope despite it being a major structural engine in
  several books (The Lies of Locke Lamora flagged this specifically).
- No vocabulary for biological/surgical (non-mechanical, non-digital)
  intelligence enhancement (Flowers for Algernon) or for an artificially
  assembled biological being gaining consciousness outside a
  mechanical/digital frame (Frankenstein's Creature) — both left
  untagged rather than stretched into `cybernetic_enhancement`/
  `ai_consciousness`.
- No content warning or trope for "in-progress societal collapse
  experienced directly by refugees," distinct from the sudden single-
  cataclysm shape `sudden_apocalypse_event` was built for (Parable of
  the Sower's slower climate/economic unraveling was a slightly awkward
  fit for it).

None of these were applied in this pass — logged here for the same
deliberate review discipline as every other vocabulary decision in this
project.
